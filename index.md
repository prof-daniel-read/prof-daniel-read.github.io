My research spans experimental economics, judgment and decision making, and behavioural microeconomics. Much of it concerns how people make choices over time — why we are impatient, how we trade off sooner against later outcomes, and how such choices should be modelled — alongside work on deception, nudging, and behaviour change.

I teach behavioural microeconomics and negotiation, and supervise PhD students. I am an associate editor at *Management Science*.

Before joining Warwick in 2011 I was Professor of Behavioural Economics at Durham Business School, Reader in Operational Research at the LSE, and a lecturer at Leeds University Business School, with visiting positions at Yale and INSEAD. I received my PhD in psychology from the University of Toronto.
